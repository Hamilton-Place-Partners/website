My approach

What I look for.

The business and how it operates

  • What the business needs next.

    Where it’s heading, what the organization has to deliver, and at what cost.

  • How the work gets done.

    How work flows end to end, what to cut, simplify or automate, and where judgment is needed.

  • How the organization is structured and resourced.

    Structure, spans and layers, what to own, share or source outside, and what each option costs.

People, decisions and performance

  • What each role is for.

    Purpose first, then tasks. The tasks that look routine often carry the judgment that makes the role valuable, and need protection from being cut or automated by mistake.

  • Who decides, who owns the result, and how it’s checked.

    Where decisions sit, where they get stuck, and who answers for each outcome, including work done by AI. Checks are designed into the work, because people trying harder doesn’t scale.

  • What the measures reward.

    People follow what gets measured and recognized. If the measures stay the same, the organization drifts back to the old way.

  • Which capabilities to build, and how people still learn.

    What to own, build or source, and how people still build expertise when the work that taught them changes.

  • Whether leaders and managers can lead it.

    Defining what good looks like, assessing what comes back, keeping the team’s judgment sharp, and building trust that brings problems to the surface early.

Making it work in practice

  • The safeguards nobody wrote down.

    The informal checks, connections and knowledge that keep the organization working. Not all of them should be kept: some are workarounds for a flawed design. The task is to tell which is which.

  • The transition.

    Sequence, risks, ownership and measures, tracked against the business case and adjusted along the way.

Two transformations

One about cost. One about growth.

Siemens Healthineers
A global supply chain reset
The challenge

Take significant cost out of a multi-country supply chain without losing the ability to deliver.

What I did

Led the global HR & change workstream across six supply chain function workstreams and 30+ projects: organization design, spans and layers, restructuring, strategic workforce planning for site consolidation and location expansion strategy. Forecast skills and capacity needs, and modeled the cost scenarios. Built the retention risk framework that protected critical roles, and the HR dashboards for steering committee tracking.

The result

The program exceeded its initial $280M savings target.

Pearson
A commercial organization built for growth
The challenge

A U.S. business with about $700M in revenue, growing again after years of contraction and needing to win share in a competitive market.

What I did

As HR partner to the Chief Revenue Officer, led the people strategy as the operating model evolved, including how sales and customer success teams were restructured to better align resources to revenue impact and scale account coverage and support without customer disruption.

The result

57 net-new roles built out in one quarter, the team in scope scaled by 16%.
